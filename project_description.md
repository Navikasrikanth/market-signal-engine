# SITREP — what it is

**A watchlist that tells you what you missed.**

Most watchlists show you prices. If you have been away a week, you are handed
twenty numbers and left to work out which ones matter. SITREP does that work
for you.

When you come back, it remembers exactly when you last looked and reports only
what genuinely changed since — usually three or four names, not twenty. For
each one it says what happened, when it happened, how unusual it was for that
particular company, and what it means for you: a fall is bad news on something
you own and good news on something you were waiting to buy.

It also shows its reasoning. Every name comes with the arithmetic behind why it
was ranked where it was, including what held it *back* — and the system
publishes how often its own warnings actually preceded a real move, so you can
judge whether to trust it.

Nothing is guessed or generated. Every sentence traces to a calculation.

---

## What is being summarised

- **What changed since your last visit** — not since yesterday, since *you*
- **How unusual it was** for that specific company, not in general
- **The path, not just the endpoint** — "up 5%, having been 19% higher"
- **What it means for your position** — holding, considering, hedging
- **What came and went** while you were away and has already resolved
- **What was deliberately held back**, counted honestly rather than hidden

## Why it helps

You stop scanning and start reading. The product's value is in what it
**refuses** to show you: it filters hard, tells you exactly how much it filtered,
and lets you look past the filter whenever you want.
